02 / WHAT'S UNDER THE HOUSE?

Why the risk is so different

These are educational, property-level comparisons—not engineering inspections or official hazard determinations.

VERY HIGH

189 Kent Road · Pacifica

Why this property has very high landslide risk

  • Documented landslide damage: The property listing explicitly reports major foundation and structural issues related to landslide activity.
  • Geotechnical warning: The seller's geotechnical engineers reportedly do not recommend occupying or renting the property.
  • Coastal slope setting: Pedro Point is a coastal bluff and hillside environment exposed to erosion and slope instability. Pacifica planning documents identify bluff erosion and landslides among local hazards.
  • Rainfall trigger: A nearby NOAA climate station, Pacifica 4 SSE, records about 31.88 inches of normal annual precipitation. December averages 6.64 inches. Saturated soil can add weight and reduce slope strength.
  • Coastal erosion: Ocean waves can wear away the base of coastal cliffs. This can remove support from the slope and contribute to instability over time.
  • Mitigation: No completed, verified stabilization system is established by the public listing. The disclosed structural damage means buyers should not assume repairs or retaining structures have made it safe.

The listing's own geotechnical disclosure is the decisive evidence. This is a very high-risk example, not merely a home with a scenic hillside view.

VERY LOW

4328 62nd Street · Sacramento

Why this property has very low landslide risk

  • Relatively flat terrain: Sacramento is located in the relatively flat Sacramento Valley. This property is in an established residential neighborhood rather than on a steep hillside or coastal bluff.
  • Low slope instability: Landslides generally require a slope or unstable mass of earth that can move downhill. The relatively level setting gives this property much less landslide exposure than the Pacifica home.
  • City assessment: The City of Sacramento's Climate Vulnerability Assessment states that landslides are not a major threat to the city because of its flat terrain.
  • Rainfall comparison: Sacramento receives approximately 19.2 inches of average annual precipitation, compared with about 31.88 inches at the Pacifica NOAA station. Rainfall alone does not determine landslide risk, but it is useful context.
  • Property listing: The available listing information does not report landslide-related foundation or structural damage. This is not proof that no damage exists, but it contrasts with the explicit disclosure for the Pacifica property.
  • Mitigation: No special landslide stabilization system is documented in the public listing. The low comparative risk is mainly due to the naturally level setting, not a claimed engineered repair.

The property's relatively flat setting and the city's assessment support a very low comparative landslide-risk rating. Normal home inspections and drainage checks are still important.

A CLOSER LOOK AT RAINFALL

Water matters, but terrain matters too.

Pacifica's nearby NOAA station has a wetter normal year than Sacramento. However, the most important contrast is that the Pacifica property has known landslide-related structural damage on coastal terrain, while the Sacramento property sits in a much flatter urban setting. A storm can trigger movement where unstable slopes already exist; rain by itself is not a landslide-risk rating.

03 / THE TAKEAWAY

Don't just look at the view.
Investigate the ground.

189 Kent Road has a spectacular oceanfront setting, but the reported landslide damage and geotechnical warning make it a very high-risk property. Its low asking price should not be mistaken for a bargain without major professional investigation.

4328 62nd Street is less dramatic, but its relatively level location and lack of disclosed landslide damage support a very low comparative landslide-risk rating. Buyers should still inspect drainage, foundation condition, flood exposure, and local hazard maps.

The comparison weighs (1) direct property disclosures and geotechnical findings, (2) slope and coastal setting, (3) regional landslide mapping and city planning information, and (4) rainfall as a possible trigger. The Pacifica rating is supported by explicit evidence of landslide-related damage and a non-occupancy recommendation. The Sacramento rating is a relative, regional assessment based on flat terrain and citywide findings. A licensed geotechnical engineer would need to evaluate either parcel to make a site-specific determination.
